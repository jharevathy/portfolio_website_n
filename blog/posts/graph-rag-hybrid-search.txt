## Where naive RAG breaks

A policy assistant was asked which approvals a contractor needs before getting VPN access. The answer lived in three documents that never mention each other by name. Top-k vector search returned the most similar paragraph and missed the other two.

That is the core limit: **embeddings measure resemblance**, and multi-hop questions need relationships.

> Similarity is not the same thing as *relevance.*

## Three retrievers, one ranking

- **BM25** catches exact terms such as policy IDs and product codes.
- **Dense vectors** catch paraphrases and synonyms.
- **ColBERT** scores token by token, which keeps precision on long passages.

Reciprocal rank fusion merges the three lists without tuning score scales.

```python fusion.py
def rrf(rankings, k=60):
    scores = {}
    for ranking in rankings:
        for rank, doc_id in enumerate(ranking):
            scores[doc_id] = scores.get(doc_id, 0) + 1 / (k + rank + 1)
    return sorted(scores, key=scores.get, reverse=True)
```

## Adding the graph

Entities and their relations are extracted once at indexing time. At query time the best hits become entry points, and the graph pulls in their one-hop neighbours as extra context.

Tip: Expanding two hops usually floods the prompt. One hop with a relevance cut-off gave the best answers for the least tokens.

## What changed in production

Multi-hop questions stopped failing silently, and answers started citing every document they relied on. Latency rose slightly, which a small re-ranker cache paid back.
