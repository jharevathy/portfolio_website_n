## Why we needed a judge

We were shipping prompt changes weekly and couldn't hand-review thousands of answers. An LLM judge promised fast, cheap evaluation, until we noticed it preferred whichever answer came first.

## Position bias

Run every comparison twice with the answers swapped. If the verdict flips, record a tie.

```python judge.py
a_first = judge(question, answer_a, answer_b)
b_first = judge(question, answer_b, answer_a)
verdict = a_first if a_first == flip(b_first) else "tie"
```

## Verbosity and self-preference

Judges reward longer answers and answers written in their own style. A strict rubric with explicit length guidance fixes the first. Using a different model family as judge reduces the second.

> A judge you haven't calibrated is just *another opinion.*

Note: Have people grade 200 examples, then measure how often the judge agrees before trusting it on the rest.
